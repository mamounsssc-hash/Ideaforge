You wake up tomorrow and you cannot die.

Not figuratively. Not philosophically. Biologically, permanently, you will not age, you will not decay, and no disease will ever reach you.

At first it sounds like a gift. You watch your daughter grow up. You hold your wife's hand through decades you were never supposed to see. You outlive every fear you ever had.

But then Year 30 arrives. And Year 75. And Year 200.

And you begin to understand: immortality was never about living forever. It was about losing everything — forever.

This video follows one man across 1,000 years of immortal life. From the first morning of disbelief, through the weddings and funerals, the lovers who age and the friends who disappear, the centuries of therapy and the slow erosion of identity — to the final question no one thinks to ask at the beginning:

Would you take it?

What this covers:

- The biological science of aging: telomeres, cellular senescence, and what it would actually take to stop the clock
- Hayflick's Limit and the 50-division countdown inside every human cell
- Yamanaka factors and the real experiments reversing age in mice (Sinclair Lab, Harvard, 2020)
- The psychological cost: how memory, identity, and grief function across centuries
- The economics of immortality — overpopulation, wealth inequality, and a world that was never designed for people who don't die
- The philosophy of meaning: does life matter if it never ends?
- Why every civilization that imagined immortality also imagined it as a curse
