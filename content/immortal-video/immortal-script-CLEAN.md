# "What If You Woke Up Tomorrow and Could Never Die?"

---

**COLD OPEN**

You wake up tomorrow, and you cannot die. Ever. Not in a car crash. Not from cancer. Not from old age. Nothing. You just keep going. Sounds amazing, right? Ask me again in three hundred years. Because here is the part no one warns you about. Immortality does not start with a superpower. It starts with a doctor's appointment. And it ends with you, alone, in a universe that forgot your name. But we will get there. First, let us enjoy the good part. Because the good part is really, really good.

I am going to walk you through a thousand years of your life. Year by year. And I need to warn you. The exact moment this stops being fun is the moment you are going to learn something about yourself you did not want to know. Year one.

---

**ACT 1 — THE GIFT (Year 1 – 50)**

Nothing changes. You go to work. You eat lunch. You come home. You do not feel immortal. You feel normal. Year five. A cut closes overnight. A cold lasts two hours. You think you are just healthy. Year ten. Your friends are getting gray hair and bad knees. You still look twenty-five. People say you have good genes. You laugh and say thanks.

Year twenty-five. You are fifty years old. You look twenty-five. Your daughter is twenty-two. You look younger than your own child. People assume you are her brother. You are not flattered. You are terrified. Because you realize something no doctor can explain. You are not aging. At all. Not a single day. And that is when the first crack appears. Not in your body. In your family.

Year thirty. Your wife is fifty-five. Lines around her eyes. Hair half white. She is beautiful to you. But strangers ask if she is your mother. She laughs about it. Then one night she stops laughing. She looks at you in the mirror and says, very quietly, "This is not fair." And she is right. It is not.

Year fifty. The world finds out. Overnight you become the most famous person alive. Every government wants your blood. Literally. Seven hundred samples in one year. They find nothing. You are biologically twenty-five, chronologically seventy-five, and emotionally, you are already starting to break. Because you can feel what is coming. And you cannot stop it.

---

**THE FIRST DEATH**

Year seventy-five. Your wife dies. You are at her funeral. You look like the youngest person in the room. Your granddaughter tugs your sleeve and whispers, "Are you the man from TV?" You stand at the grave of the woman you loved for fifty years, and you do not look a day over twenty-five. The cameras outside the cemetery outnumber the mourners. You want to scream. But the whole world is watching. And they think this is entertainment.

Stop. I need to tell you something. That was year seventy-five. You have nine hundred and twenty-five years left. And I promise you, it gets so much worse. Because losing your wife? That was the last time you will ever cry. Not because you become brave. Because after enough funerals, you simply run out of tears. But I am getting ahead of myself.

---

**ACT 2 — THE FADE (Year 80 – 200)**

Year eighty. You fall in love again. You know exactly how this will end. You do it anyway. Humans are not logical. Even the immortal ones.

Year one hundred. The last person who knew you when you were just a normal person has died. Every human on Earth who ever saw you as just a friend, a neighbor, a regular man, is gone. You are not a person anymore. You are a symbol. A headline. A thing people argue about online.

Year one hundred and fifty. Your retirement fund, the one you panicked about every April, is now worth more than some countries. Compound interest over a hundred and fifty years turns a modest savings account into an obscene fortune. Money means nothing to you. You have watched three economic systems collapse. You were rich in all of them.

Year two hundred. You go on a date. She asks how old you are. You say "mid-thirties." Technically, you have been mid-thirties for a hundred and seventy-five years. She asks what you do. You say, "I have done everything." She thinks you are being dramatic. You are being literal.

---

**ACT 3 — THE WEIGHT (Year 250 – 400)**

Year two hundred and fifty. You speak four languages nobody else speaks anymore. You remember countries that no longer exist. You mention a song and nobody knows what you are talking about. Your memories are becoming a private museum that only you can visit. And the museum keeps getting bigger while the number of visitors stays at one.

Year three hundred. You have had three families. You have loved three people completely. All three are dead. Their children are dead. Their grandchildren are dead. You have attended so many funerals that you stopped counting at two hundred. You stopped crying at funeral number twelve. You are not heartless. You are just out of tears.

Year three hundred and fifty. You try to die. I am sorry, that is not funny. But in a way it is, because you try everything. You jump. You drown. You walk into traffic. You just wake up. Every single time. Your body will not let you leave. You are a prisoner inside a machine that refuses to stop running.

You wonder if you are in hell. You are serious.

Year four hundred. Fifty years of therapy. Your therapist retired. Then his replacement retired. Then her replacement retired. You have outlived four therapists. But something in the last one's words stuck. She said, "You are not cursed with life. Everyone else is cursed with death. The question is what you do with the only thing nobody else has. Time."

---

**MIDPOINT — THIS IS NOT SCIENCE FICTION**

Now here is the part that should genuinely unsettle you. Because everything I just described is not as far away as you think. Right now, a man named Bryan Johnson spends two million dollars a year trying to reverse his own aging. His biological age is measurably younger than his real age. In 2012, a scientist named Shinya Yamanaka won the Nobel Prize for discovering that old cells can be reprogrammed back to a young state. In 2023, researchers at Harvard reversed aging in mice. Not slowed it. Reversed it. The mice got younger. The technology is called cellular reprogramming. It works. It is getting cheaper. And nobody voted on whether humanity should have it. The first human who lives to two hundred may already be alive. The question is not whether immortality will arrive. The question is whether you are ready for what it actually looks like.

---

**ACT 4 — THE STRANGER (Year 500 – 1,000)**

Year five hundred. You are alive. But are you still you? You cannot remember your mother's face. You know you had one. You know you loved her. But ten thousand faces have passed through your life since then, and hers was swallowed by the flood. You try to feel something about this. You feel nothing. And that feeling of nothing is the most terrifying thing that has ever happened to you.

Year six hundred. Your original language has changed so much that if you spoke it the way you learned it, no one alive would understand you. You are a living fossil. A walking dictionary of a dead world.

Year seven hundred. The world has split into two camps. People who want your immortality. And people who want you dead because of it. You have survived six assassination attempts. You have watched religions form around you. In one country they worship you. In the country next door, they burn images of you. You did not ask for any of it. You just wanted to eat lunch.

Year one thousand. You have been alive for ten centuries. You have watched the rise and fall of nations the way other people watch seasons change. Spring. Summer. A civilization. Winter. Another one gone. You remember all of them. Nobody else does.

---

**ACT 5 — THE REAL COST**

Here is the thing almost nobody thinks about when they imagine immortality. It is not the loneliness. It is not the boredom. It is not even the grief. It is the fact that after a thousand years, you are no longer human. Not because your body changed. Because your mind did. A human mind was built to hold eighty years of experience. You are carrying a thousand. The weight does not make you wise. It makes you alien. You think in centuries. Everyone around you thinks in weekends. You have seen every mistake humanity makes, because humanity keeps making the same four mistakes on a loop. And you cannot warn them. Because they never listen. Because no one listens to someone who has been right for a thousand years. They just call you arrogant and move on.

---

**ENDING**

So now I have to ask you the only question that matters. After everything you just heard. The love you will lose. The identity you will forget. The centuries of grief you cannot escape. The world that will worship you and hunt you in the same breath. Would you still take it?

Be honest. Because here is what the scientists have not figured out yet. They are working on how to stop your body from dying. Nobody is working on how to stop your mind from breaking.

And the strangest part? I already know your answer. I know because you clicked on this video. You sat through ten minutes of warnings. And right now, a small voice in your head is whispering, "Yeah. But I would handle it differently."

That is exactly what every immortal says. Right before year three hundred.

If someone offered you immortality tonight, with one condition, you can never turn it off, would you take it? Tell me in the comments. I actually want to know.
