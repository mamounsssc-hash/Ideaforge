**COLD OPEN — THE HOOK**
A thousand years from now, a human is going to look at a photo of you. And feel a little sick. Not because of your face. Because of your body. To them, you look like a rough draft. Almost an animal. The same way you look at a caveman chewing on a bone. And here is the uncomfortable part. They are right. Your body is primitive. And it is already being replaced. Not in some far-off lab. Right now. While you sit here watching this, at least four different forces are quietly redrawing the human body. One of them is working inside your eyes at this exact second. Give me the next few minutes, and you will be able to point at the specific parts of you that will not survive the next thousand years. The eyes. The face. The bones. So what will humans actually look like in the year 3000? Not in a movie. In real biology, based on things happening to your body today. Let's start with the one you can feel right now.

**ACT 1 — THE SCREEN EYE**
In 2016, researchers at the Brien Holden Vision Institute published a number that stunned them. By the year 2050, they expect half the planet to be short-sighted. Five billion people, in a single generation. Here is why it is happening. Think about how close everything you look at really is. A phone, forty centimeters from your face. A screen. A desk. A wall across the room. For almost all of human history, our eyes watched the horizon. Hunting. Tracking movement far away. Staring into the distance for hours. Your eye was built for that world. Now a child spends the entire day looking up close, indoors, under a light that never existed in nature. And the eye reacts to that. When you focus up close for years, while the eye is still growing, the eyeball slowly stretches longer. That stretched shape is what short-sightedness actually is. It is not a defect. It is your body remodeling itself for a world made of screens. In some cities in East Asia, more than eight out of ten teenagers finish school already short-sighted. Now take that pressure and run it for a thousand years. The normal human eye may simply be built for close range. And sharp distance vision, the thing that kept your ancestors alive, becomes a rare, lucky trait. And the eye is one of the slowest parts of you to change. Your face is moving faster.

**ACT 2 — THE SHRINKING FACE** 😄
Open your mouth and run your tongue along the back of your teeth. If you still have your wisdom teeth, they probably hurt coming in. If a dentist pulled them out, that is the whole point. Those teeth are a leftover from an ancestor with a much bigger jaw. For most of human history, we chewed raw plants, tough roots, and uncooked meat for hours every day. That work built a large, strong jaw, with room for every tooth. Then we learned to cook. Cooked food is soft. Soft food does not need a big jaw. So over thousands of years, the human jaw quietly shrank. But the teeth never got the message. Your wisdom teeth are basically your body trying to install a mouth you no longer have. There is not enough room, so they come in sideways, or never come in at all. More and more people today are simply born without wisdom teeth ever forming. Push that a thousand years forward, and the future human may have a smaller, flatter face, a delicate jaw, and a neat set of teeth with nothing left over. Softer food, smaller face. Your descendants might look at your jaw the way you look at a bulldog's. But your body does not only respond to what you eat. It responds to the sky.

**ACT 3 — THE WARMER BODY**
Your body answers the temperature around it. And the planet is getting warmer. There is a simple, old pattern in biology. Animals in cold places tend to be short and stocky, to hold their heat in. Animals in hot places tend to be lean and long, to let their heat out. Humans already follow it. People whose ancestors come from cold northern lands tend to be broader and rounder. People from hot regions near the equator tend to be taller and thinner. Same body, tuned to two different thermostats. Now push the whole planet a few degrees warmer, over centuries. The body that sheds heat best is the body most likely to survive and have children. Longer arms and legs. A leaner frame. More skin surface to release warmth through. The human of the future may be clearly taller and thinner than you, not because it looks better on camera, but because it does not overheat. That is old, slow evolution, doing what it has always done, quietly, across generations. But something brand new just walked into the room. And this one does not wait for generations. For the first time, humans have grabbed the pen, and started editing the code by hand.

**ACT 4 — THE ONE FACE**
Look at a crowded street for a second. Every skin tone. Every kind of hair. Every shape of eye. We treat these differences as huge, and permanent. They are neither. Skin color is mostly just a response to sunlight. Strong sun near the equator built darker skin to protect the body. Weaker sun far to the north favored lighter skin, to soak up what little sun there was. That is almost the entire story. It is a sunscreen setting, not a border. And for the first time in human history, those settings are mixing. For hundreds of thousands of years, people lived and had children within a few days' walk of where they were born. Today, a person born on one continent can start a family with someone born on the far side of the planet, and that is becoming completely ordinary. Over enough generations, all that variation blends toward the middle. A thousand years from now, the sharp categories people argue about today may melt into a single, brown-toned, mixed human, carrying features pulled from every corner of the map. The strange part is not that we will all look different. It is that we might slowly start to look the same. But even that is nothing next to what happens when people stop leaving their children to chance.

**ACT 5 — THE EDITED HUMAN** 😄
In November 2018, a scientist in China named He Jiankui told the world something it was not ready for. Twin girls had just been born from embryos he had edited before birth, using a tool called CRISPR, to change one of their genes. The first gene-edited humans in history. They are alive right now, walking around somewhere. The world was furious. He went to prison. But the line was already crossed. And a line like that does not un-cross. The tool works. And it gets cheaper and more precise every single year. So this does not arrive as some scary science-fiction jump. It arrives quietly, one reasonable step at a time. First, a parent removes a deadly disease from their baby before it is even born. Almost nobody argues with that. Then, why not fix the eyesight while you are in there. Then height. Then how slowly the body ages. Want a child who never needs glasses and actually sleeps through the night? Give it a few decades. There will be a menu. Probably with a subscription. And every generation stacks its edits on top of the one before. Run that for a thousand years, and you do not get a person who looks a little different from you. You get a person who was designed. On purpose. By someone.

**ACT 6 — THE BODY THAT LEFT EARTH** 😄
And some humans will not be shaped by Earth at all. NASA once ran an experiment that sounds invented, but it is completely real. They had two brothers who were both astronauts, and happened to be identical twins. Scott and Mark Kelly. They sent one of them to space for almost a year, and kept the other one on the ground. Same genes. Two different worlds. When Scott came back, his body had changed. He was actually taller for a while, because with no gravity pulling down on it, his spine had stretched out. His heart changed shape. His eyesight changed. Even the way his genes switched on and off had shifted. And that was only a few months. Now picture humans born and raised on Mars, in one third of Earth's gravity, generation after generation. Their bodies would never be pulled down the way yours is, every second of your life. They would grow up taller. Thinner. With lighter, weaker bones, because weak bones are all that weak gravity ever asks for. A Mars-born human might look incredible standing on Mars. They just could not survive the trip to visit family on Earth. Their own skeleton would crack under the weight of a planet they came from. Within a few centuries, the human body could quietly split into two. One built for Earth. And one that can never come home.

**MIDPOINT — YOU ARE THE OLD MODEL**
Okay. Now the part that flips this entire video around. We keep describing future humans like they are the weird ones. The tall thin ones. The ones with the redesigned eyes, the shrunken jaw, the chosen genes, the Martian bones. So stand inside their world for one second. Turn around. And look back at us. To them, you are the rough draft. You are the one still carrying disease genes nobody removed. Eyes that only worked by luck. A jaw stuffed with teeth that do not fit. A body shaped by pure accident, instead of by choice. They would look at a photo of you the exact same way you look at a hunched caveman gnawing on a bone. Not with hate. With a little bit of pity. That was us, they will say. Before we learned how to fix it. And here is the thing almost nobody wants to say out loud. There may be no final human. Ever. Every single version of us thinks it is the finished one. The caveman thought it. You think it right now, watching this. And you are simply the model they will one day put behind glass.

**ACT 7 — THE CHOICE NOBODY VOTED ON**
There is one last thing, and it is the heaviest one in this whole video. For four billion years, evolution never asked permission. It had no plan, no opinion, no meeting on a Monday morning. It just kept whatever survived, and threw the rest away. What is happening now is completely different. For the first time, the future shape of the human body is turning into a choice. An actual decision. Made by scientists. By companies. By parents scrolling through a list of traits before their child is even born. Nobody ever held a vote on what our species should become. There is no committee for the human of the year 3000. It will get built the way almost everything gets built. A little at a time. By whoever can afford the tools first. For reasons that feel completely obvious in the moment. And a thousand years from now, someone will wake up inside the result of all those tiny choices, and simply assume it was always meant to look this way.

**ENDING — ROLE REVERSAL**
So picture the two of you, standing in the same room. A human from the year 3000. Tall, lean, calm. Eyes and genes chosen before they were born. A body that has never felt a single one of the diseases you live with. And then you. With your accidental eyes. Your unedited genes. Your ordinary, five-fingered hands. They would study you like something behind glass in a museum, amazed you survived at all with a body that literally no one designed. And you would look back at them, and quietly wonder if anything human was still left in there, or if it had all been polished away. Here is the truth. Neither of you is the real human. You are two frames from the same long film. One near the beginning. One much further in. And the film is still rolling. Right now. In your eyes, straining at this bright screen up close. In a sky getting slowly warmer above you. In a lab, tonight, where someone is learning to spell in the language of genes. So look down at your hand one more time. It was never the final version. It never will be. And if you could change one thing about the human body, and hand it to the people of the year 3000, what would you change? Tell me in the comments. I actually want to know.
